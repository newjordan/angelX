# The Teachers of the Realm

> Level 3 and above of any wish or skill is never found, bought, or simply
> wished for. A knight earns it: the power's teacher must be found in the
> realm, and their questline walked to its end. Levels 1 and 2 stay as they
> are — wishes and cards, gated by the school's ranks. This document is the
> builders' bible for that rule. It contradicts nothing in
> [DELVE-LORE.md](DELVE-LORE.md), [KINGDOM.md](KINGDOM.md),
> [DELVE.md](DELVE.md) or [world-adventure.md](world-adventure.md).

## The spine: the Fellowship of the Carried Fire

Before the Delve had a name, before the Undercroft was a cellar, there was a
company called the Fellowship of the Carried Fire. They were the realm's first
knight-adventurers, and between them they held every power the Delve now
grants in pieces. The tales disagree on whether they went down after the
Grail, or after something that had taken the Grail's shape; what the tales
agree on is this: the company came back up. Every one of them. But they did
not come back whole. Each of them had left something down there — and what
each left behind, the realm had given back to them in the shape of a gift.
When the Fellowship broke — not in battle, but over a quarrel about whether
to go down again — each member carried away one power, kept whole where the
realm now grants it in thirds.

They did not hide the powers. They simply stopped teaching them. A power held
whole by one who earned it cannot be stolen, wished for, or found in a coffer;
it can only be given, by the one who holds it, to one who walks the same road
they walked. That is why level 3 is taught and never found. The teachers are
not hoarding. They are the lock and the key both: the last keepers of the
whole gift, waiting for a knight who will come to them the way they once came
to their own teachers — with the power at its second rank already burning in
the hand, and a question.

Old Blaise has written of them in the margin of every page of the Delve's
first book: *"The Fellowship quarrelled over going down again. Twenty-two of
them stayed up. One went down alone. The realm still owes that one a
knight."* The Grail quest is the same road. The stone rolls back down every
night; the Fellowship learned that too, laughed, and pushed it again. Their
questlines are not errands. They are the same hill, seen from twenty-two
sides.

The Fellowship's mark is three small flames arranged in a triangle, points
inward, drawn wherever a teacher can be found. Wren, who draws the map as you
go, has begun noticing the mark on walls, doors and signposts around the
realm, and pins each new one to her board by the Winding Stair. Nobody in the
realm can read it yet except the teachers themselves.

When a questline is finished, the teacher takes a seat at the Round Table —
the room, not the furniture — as the Fellowship's charter says: *the Table
has twenty-two seats, and no seat is filled until its teacher has passed the
power on.* The Table is not yet full. The realm is counting.

## The roster: twenty-two seats

One teacher per power. The seventeen wishes that already have a third tier,
the two that are given one here (Twin Fletch and Awakened Fork — their new
card lines and the caps they need are in the builders' notes at the end),
and the five skill-bar skills, whose levels I–III are proposed in their
entries. A teacher will not speak of the whole gift until the knight stands
before them holding that power's second rank — that is the rule's gate, and
it is why no teacher is ever "found" early by accident.

### The wish teachers

**Sela Three-Arrows — Triple Volley.** The Old Watch's last scout-captain,
who held the north woods when the Fellowship still marched, and who still
climbs the tower each dusk "because the view hasn't resigned." She lives in
the **Old Watch** tower in the Woods, the bell back in its arch. Look: a
lean woman in a watch-coat gone green at the seams, three goose-fletched
arrows in her hatband, never fewer — she counts them aloud when nervous.
Lines: *"One arrow is a question. Three is a conversation."* / *"Loose
them together and they argue over who lands first. Feed them first, then
loose."*

**Old Carom — Ricochet.** The Fellowship's siege-gamer, who won three
castles at table and gave them all back because "the games were better than
the winning." He lives in the **skittle alley beside Maud's taps** in the
Siege Perilous, once the west wing is dug out and Maud is behind the bar.
Look: a tiny man with chalk dust permanently on his cuffs, polishing a
wooden ball that squeaks. Lines: *"A wall is a friend who passes the
message on."* / *"You missed. Wonderful. Now watch what the wall does
with it."*

**Fenna the Fletcher — Seeker Fletching.** The Eyrie's arrow-mistress, who
fletched for the whole Fellowship and says the whole Fellowship still owes
her for the arrows they didn't bring back. She lives at the **Eyrie**, in
the high rookery among the mews. Look: a hawk-feather cloak, a whittling
knife behind one ear, and the habit of sniffing an arrow before she
fletches it. Lines: *"An arrow wants to please. Give it one monster to
love."* / *"It isn't magic. The arrow simply listens better than you do."*

**Gil the Ley-Lighter — Chain Spark.** The lamplighter of the old roads,
who kept every lamp between the Watch and the priory lit for forty years
and came to think of sparks as a postal service. He lives along the
**Lamplighter's Walk**, once the Wayfarer's Beacon is built and the old
watch's bell recovered. Look: a brass-buttoned coat, a long pole with one
stub of candle, and a faint smell of pitch that arrives before he does.
Lines: *"Every lamp is lit from the one before it. That's all a spark
asks."* / *"Stand in the wet and complain, or stand in the wet and
conduct."*

**Dame Alyce — Mending Strike.** The priory's infirmarer, who patched the
Fellowship through everything and billed them in honey. She lives at
**Puddingstone Priory** on the High Roads, in the little infirmary that
smells of clean linen and ambition. Look: sleeves rolled, a satchel of
plasters, and a laugh the colour of honey. Lines: *"Hit them gently, hit
them often — I mean the monsters, dear."* / *"A wound is only a mending
that hasn't been introduced yet."*

**Master Wymond — Bodkin Heads.** The Smithy's old point-forger, who makes
arrowheads the way other people make arguments: narrow, patient, and
impossible to wave away. He lives at the **forges of Caer Dwfn**, King
Brannoc's smithy at the mine-head. Look: burn-scarred forearms, a squint
that works in the dark, and a wax tablet of his own thumbprints for testing
points. Lines: *"Armour is a door. I make the polite knock that opens
it."* / *"Bodkin. It means the little awl that says please."*

**Pib — Quick Hands.** Fool's Green's plate-spinner, the only mummer the
Fellowship ever took seriously, mostly because he stole their wallets to
prove a point and gave them back with interest. He lives in **Mummers'
Hollow** at the Fair. Look: motley gone grey at the elbows, six tin plates
in a stack, and a wink that uses both eyes at once. Lines: *"Slow is just
fast with a limp."* / *"Two hands, four plates, no excuses — right, now
juggle the cabbage."*

**Old Sledge — Heavy Heads.** A dwarf of the old galleries who believes
lightness is a phase people grow out of. He keeps the **counterweight shop
at Gullible Cliffs**, where the goat lift is wound. Look: barrel-chested,
a hammer worn down to a wedge, tea the colour of ore. Lines: *"Heavy is
honest. Light has to explain itself."* / *"Swing once like you mean it,
and the wall will thank you personally."*

**Reynard the Post — Fleet Foot.** The realm's retired courier, who
carried the Fellowship's letters and once outran an avalanche to deliver a
birthday card on time (it was late; he still disputes this). He lives at
the **Compliment Toll Bridge** booth in the Tilt-Yards. Look: a coat of
sewn shoe-leather, letters in his hatband, and a way of standing while
sitting. Lines: *"The goose is faster than you, and it knows it.
Insulting, isn't it?"* / *"Arrive. That's the whole trick. Arrive."*

**Goodwife Barley — Stout Heart.** The baker of the **Goose-Proof Bakery
at Wonky Windmill**, who has been charged by the same goose for forty years
and considers the relationship stable. Look: forearms like loaves, a
floured apron, and a stance like bread proving. Lines: *"Stand like bread
proving — unmoved, and rising all the while."* / *"The goose has charged
me for forty years. I am still the bakery."*

**Sir Ferris the Kettle — Iron Skin.** A knight of the Fellowship who has
not taken his armour off since the quarrel, on the grounds that it fits.
He lodges at **the lists**, east of the gate, in the stands' shadow. Look:
full plate gone teakettle-brown, dents like a map of the moon, and the
habit of hanging his helm on empty air. Lines: *"I haven't seen my elbows
in thirty years and I have never once been sorry."* / *"Let it hit you.
Politely. Like collecting a toll."*

**Maren of the Deepwater — Blood Drinker.** The deep end of every web. A
pale scholar who keeps a dry study in the **Drowned Archive** floor of the
Delve, in a room the water has agreed to respect. She held the Fellowship's
whole gift for mending-from-monsters, and kept a ledger of every drop she
ever borrowed; the ledger is balanced, and she will show you. Look: a dry
robe in a wet place, spectacles, and a ledger bound in counted pages.
Lines: *"I borrow. I always return. Check the ledger — it's a good
ledger."* / *"Life is a currency, knight. Spend a monster's folly, never
a friend's future."*

**Dame Petronel — Morningstar.** The Whirligig of the **Rusty
Hippodrome**, who danced for the Fellowship's victories and fought for
their retreats, and saw no contradiction. She lives above the track's
silver-railed curve. Look: tiny, silver plaits wound with flail-chain, and
a body that keeps perfect time with everything, including you. Lines:
*"Two stars, one orbit, no negotiation."* / *"They call it a defensive
weapon. Watch my defense remove their argument."*

**Old Fuse — Blasting Powder.** Powder-keeper of **Saltspite Works** on
the Coast, who lost his eyebrows to the cause of knowledge and considers
them well spent. Look: no eyebrows at all, a trailing smell of matches,
and fingers kept wet for counting. Lines: *"Measure twice, ignite once,
apologize to the crabs later."* / *"A quarter in the hundred sounds
small. Ask the wall."*

**Brother Fenwick the Unashamed — Fart Bombs.** A marsh hermit of the
**Sunken Abbey's dry chapel** in the Wetland, who holds that the body is a
gift and the gift should be shared, at range. Look: a habit hitched to the
knees, an unrepentant grin, and a rosary of beans. Lines: *"The marsh was
given the same gift. I merely take notes."* / *"A knight who cannot clear
a room with dignity has never truly had dignity."*

**Hodge and Podge — Twin Fletch.** Twin fletchers, one Fellowship seat
between them (the charter was unclear and they refuse to clarify). They
live in the **stables' loft**, west of the gate. Look: identical except
that one is left-handed; they finish each other's arrows. Lines: *"Two
shafts, one thought."* / *"Which of us taught you? Yes."*

**Stilt the Heron-Warden — Awakened Fork.** A very tall, very still man
who has warded the **millpond** in the Wetland since before the sluice was
cut, and who is suspected by the badger of being three quarters heron.
Look: tall as a door, a hat brim like a beak, and a habit of standing on
one leg while the other "takes a turn off." Lines: *"One becomes two
becomes four. So does patience. So do debts."* / *"The heron throws its
beak once and eats for a week."*

### The skill teachers

**Widow Peascod — Fart Cloud.** The marshwife of the **Swamp's reed-isle**
in the Wetland, who taught the Fellowship's cloud-work and weather
generally. Look: a black shawl, billowing skirts, and a presence that
arrives slightly before she does. Lines: *"A cloud is weather you can
aim."* / *"Hold it in and it becomes philosophy. Let it go and it becomes
tactics."*

**Bess the Beaconwife — Fire Pit.** Keeper of the **beacon** on the Coast,
who lit the realm's warning fires through the Fellowship's long nights and
has strong views on flame's table manners. Look: soot-braided hair, red
petticoats, and a long taper carried like a duchess carries a fan. Lines:
*"Ground burns. It doesn't fly. Fire has manners."* / *"Feed the pit,
then feed it a cloud — that's the whole almanac."*

**Underforeman Doorne — Mines.** A dwarf of the deep galleries under the
**Mines** on the Mountain, who inherited the Fellowship's ground-work and
their superstitions, both in good order. Look: a lamp with a green shade,
steps counted aloud, and a chalked little door drawn on every tally.
Lines: *"A mine is a promise you make to the future, at the future's
expense."* / *"Never hurry in my gallery. The floor is patient, but it
prefers the slow."*

**Hesper the Hedgewife — Barrier.** Keeper of the **hedge maze** in the
Woods, who grew the Fellowship's screens and taught them that a wall is
just a hedge with opinions. Look: green to the eyes, bronze secateurs, and
a habit of being woven into her own hedge at dusk. Lines: *"A wall that
fights back is a hedge with opinions."* / *"Stand behind it and let the
monsters negotiate with wood."*

**Sir Castor of the Gatehouse — Shield Throw.** The Fellowship's doorman,
who held the gate of every hall they ever took and never once lost a
shield, because he always got it back. He lives at the **Gatehouse** in the
Delve's gate courtyard. Look: a shield polished like a mirror on both
faces, and one shoulder set lower than the other from a lifetime of it.
Lines: *"A shield travels best alone. Like gossip."* / *"Throw your wall.
Now you are the missile's problem."*

## The questlines

Every questline follows the same shape: a hook the knight hears at the
power's level-2 card, four to six steps whose deeds name real counters, a
last trial of the power itself, a reward, and ties to at least two other
questlines. A teacher will open their questline only to a knight holding
that power's second rank — the rule's gate. Steps travel the realm and use
its games: the pastimes, the races, the delve floors, the College, the
barony. Each is written to be walked in one or two evenings.

### Triple Volley — Sela Three-Arrows, the Old Watch

- **The hook:** the level-2 card gains one line: *"...and a small mark of
  three flames, points inward, scratched under the fletching."* Wren pins
  the first three-flames pin to her board the next time the knight passes
  the Winding Stair.
- **Steps:**
  1. **Watchman's rounds.** Stand a full night watch with Sela in the Old
     Watch tower: stay within the beacon's pool from dusk-bell to dawn-bell
     while three cued watches tick. *She hands you her spyglass and talks
     of the nights the Fellowship came back through the trees, three
     abreast.* Counter: `nights_watch_stood` (new, in the tower, once per
     night cycle). Her line: *"Three abreast. That's how they came home."*
  2. **The goose argument.** Win a Trencher Toss round at Goose Green
     with three catches in one round. *The goose respects nothing but
     consistency; Sela insists it is training for volley discipline.*
     Counter: `pastime_rounds_won` (existing — the realm's pastime
     records). *"Even the goose. Especially the goose."*
  3. **The Fellowship's range.** Clear the hedge maze's heart in the Woods
     while loosing only triple volleys. *At the maze's heart stand three
     old targets, one arrow each, decades old.* Counter: `volley_targets_hit`
     (new — a shot with three-wide active that lands). *"Feed them first,
     then loose."*
  4. **The quarrel, retold.** At Maud's tavern, buy Sela's old seat one
     drink and listen to the regulars' version of the night the Fellowship
     broke. *Maud's version is warmer than Sela's and neither is wrong.*
     Counter: `tavern_tales_heard` (new — one per seat's story). *"You've
     heard it from the ground now. Mine has more weather in it."*
- **The trial:** **Three abreast.** In the Delve, fell a hall's monsters
  with three-in-one-volley kills only — every killing blow must come from
  a three-wide shot, no exceptions, one hall. *Sela watches from the
  doorway, counting under her breath like she counts her arrows.* Counter:
  `volley_pure_halls` (new). *"Conversation over."*
- **The reward:** Volley tier 3 unlocked (`volley 5` — every 5th shot
  three wide). Title: **Three-Arrow**. Keepsake: **the Watchband**
  (worn): a goose-feather band for the wrist, three plumes, that plays a
  small triple plume-flick on every third volley.
- **The ties:** Sela's step 2 shares Goose Green with Goodwife Barley's
  (Stout Heart) and Pib's (Quick Hands); her tower's beacon pool is Gil's
  (Chain Spark) first lamp; step 4 is Maud's tavern, where Old Carom lives
  (Ricochet).

### Ricochet — Old Carom, the Siege Perilous

- **The hook:** the level-2 card reads *"...and a chalk cross on the
  table's edge, as if a very small man had measured the shot."* The first
  three-flames pin inside the west wing points at the skittle alley.
- **Steps:**
  1. **Chalk the line.** Return Old Carom's squeaking ball from wherever
     the realm's monsters have hidden it: a delve drop or a wanted-hall
     bounty target. *The ball squeaks in the pack the whole way home.*
     Counter: `relics_returned` (new — the teachers' keepsakes and tools
     brought home). *"You brought my argument back. Good lad."*
  2. **The banked quintain.** At the lists, strike the quintain's shield
     with a shot that has already bounced off the wall once. *The
     marshal's chalk crosses the same spot Carom would have chosen.*
     Counter: `banked_quintain_hits` (new). *"The wall passed it on. The
     wall always passes it on."*
 3. **Skittles, honestly.** Win a round of skittles at Maud's alley —
     but every toppling pin must fall to a banked ball. *The alley
     regulars begin taking notes.* Counter: `skittle_banks` (new). *"Now
     you're playing the table, not the pins."*
  4. **The debt of three castles.** Play one full game of king-and-castle
     strategy at the College of the Labyrinth's great table (Bartosz
     presiding), and lose on purpose — Carom's way of returning what he
     won. *Bartosz writes the loss into the college ledger with visible
     pleasure.* Counter: `college_games_played` (existing — the College's
     shared research quests and games). *"Losing well is a skill and I
     have never once had to teach it to a humble man."*
- **The trial:** **The Long Bank.** In one delve hall, clear the monsters
  using only shots that have bounced at least twice before striking.
  *Carom stands at the door with chalk, marking each bounce on the stone.*
  Counter: `ricochet_pure_halls` (new). *"Three walls, one argument,
  case closed."*
- **The reward:** Ricochet tier 3 (`bounce 2; damage 8`). Title: **the
  Banked Shot**. Keepsake: **the Chalk Cuff** (worn): white cuffs that
  leave a small chalk mark where a bounced shot strikes.
- **The ties:** lives beside Maud (all tavern teachers); his ball-hunt
  step shares `relics_returned` with Sela's spyglass and Sir Castor's
  gate-shield; step 2 crosses the lists with Sir Ferris (Iron Skin).

### Seeker Fletching — Fenna the Fletcher, the Eyrie

- **The hook:** the level-2 card carries *"...fletched with a feather
  that steers, from a bird that watches."* A three-flames pin appears on
  the Eyrie's mews door.
- **The grid is a ride:** the Eyrie is reached by the rookery roads and
  the mail-cart from Parliament; the questline walks it.
- **Steps:**
  1. **The mews' morning.** Feed and hood the mews' birds with Fenna:
     three cued feedings, one per roost. *The last bird takes the hood
     from your hand as if conferring a title.* Counter:
     `mews_feedings_done` (new). *"They remember hands. Be worth
     remembering."*
  2. **Signal practice.** At Signal Hill, send FISH, BOOT, CROWN to the
     lookout — the kite-post's old codes — three for three. *The lookout
     answers each with the same absurd brevity.* Counter:
     `signal_codes_sent` (existing — the lookout's FISH/BOOT/CROWN).
     *"Kites and arrows agree: go where you're sent."*
  4. **The lost quiver.** Recover the Fellowship's quiver from the Rookery
     (a wanted-hall target): any three monsters fall, the quiver is
     returned. *Each arrow in it is fletched with a different feather, one
     for each seat of the Table.* Counter: `relics_returned` (shared).
     *"Twenty-two feathers. Count them. No — count them properly."*
  4. **The arguing shot.** At the College of the Labyrinth, watch a
     homing-only run of the research labyrinth — one full circuit, no
     wrong turns — while Fenna dissects every turn. *Bartosz grades the
     circuit and gives it a name: the Arguing Shot.* Counter:
     `college_runs` (existing). *"It listens. The good ones always
     listen."*

- **The trial:** **One monster, one love.** In one delve hall, every
  monster must fall to a homing shot as its killing blow. *Fenna sniffs
  the air after each kill, like checking fletching by smell.* Counter:
  `seeker_pure_halls` (new). *"It isn't magic. It simply listens better
  than you."*
- **The reward:** Seeker Fletching tier 3 (`homing 2; damage 8`). Title:
  **the Loved Arrow**. Keepsake: **the Steering Feather** (worn): a hawk
  feather at the ear that flicks toward the nearest monster.
- **The ties:** the Rookery step shares the wanted hall with Carom's ball
  hunt (`relics_returned`); Signal Hill is shared with Gil's Chain Spark
  lamps; the College circuit with Bartosz is also Dame Petronel's step 3.

  to 3 and cap to 3.
- **The hook:** the level-2 card carries *"...and a heron's patience,
  standing on one leg."* The millpond's sluice post bears the
  three-flames mark at the waterline.
- **The fork is a heron's throw:** one beak, then two, then four — the
  heron's arithmetic.
- **Steps:**
  1. **The one-leg stand.** Stand with Stilt on the millpond's spit,

     *"One becomes two becomes four. So does patience."*
  2. **The sluice work.** Do one full round of FISH, BOOT, DUCK sluice
     work at the millpond — the ferry office's own errand. *The sluice

     (existing-shared — the sluice codes). *"The heron throws its beak
     once and eats for a week."*
  3. **The punt roundtrip.** (see Fenwick's step 2 — the same boat

  4. **The orchard rows.** Walk the orchard's rows to the bothy and

     from one. The heron eats for a week."*
- **The trial:** **The arithmetic hall.** Clear one delve hall where
  every monster falls to a fork — every killing blow a forked shot's
  child. *Stilt stands at the door on one leg, the other taking its
  turn off.* Counter: `fork_pure_halls` (new). *"One becomes two
  becomes four. So does the hall."*
- **The reward:** Awakened Fork tier 3 (`fork 3`). Title: **the
  Heron's Patience**. Keepsake: **the Waterline Band** (worn): a band
  at the ankle, wet always, that ripples once on every forked kill.
- **The ties:** the sluice and punt are the marsh's shared errands
  (Chain Spark, Fart Bombs); the orchard beans are Fenwick's tribute

## The order of things

**Where every knight begins.** The rule's gate means nobody meets a
teacher before their power's second rank, so the first teachers a new
player finds are the ones standing on the first ramble's own roads:
**Sir Castor at the Gatehouse** (every delve begins in his courtyard),
**Sela at the Old Watch, Pib at Fool's Green, Goodwife Barley at Wonky
Windmill** — three of the ramble's six places — and **the skill
teachers whose steps walk those same roads** (Peascod's reed-isle and
Bess's beacon are both day-one countryside). These five wish-lines and
five skill-lines are the web's wide mouth.

**What gates what.** Some questlines inherit the realm's own
prerequisites and say so plainly: **Old Carom** waits behind the west
wing's digging (Maud rescued, the tavern open); **Gil the Ley-Lighter**
waits behind the Wayfarer's Beacon and the recovered bell; **Maren**
waits behind two finished Fellowship questlines of any kind, and behind
the Drowned Archive's depth; **Hodge and Podge** share the stables Wat
keeps; **Bartosz's table** (Carom, Fenna, Petronel, Maren) waits only
for the College's own opening. No questline requires another
questline's *reward* — only its road, its counter or its company.

**The suggested climb, in evenings.**

1. **First evenings — the ramble's teachers:** Triple Volley, Shield
   Throw, Quick Hands, Stout Heart; the skill lines Fart Cloud and
   Barrier open here too.
2. **The circuit widens:** Fleet Foot, Iron Skin, Heavy Heads,
   Morningstar; Fire Pit and Mines open (the forge and the galleries
   are reached on these same roads).
3. **The far roads:** Bodkin Heads, Seeker Fletching, Blasting Powder,
   Twin Fletch; Shield Throw's level III closes here.
4. **The marsh and the wet roads:** Mending Strike, Fart Bombs,
   Awakened Fork, Chain Spark (once the beacon is built).
5. **The deep end — always last:** **Blood Drinker.** Maren's door
   opens only to a knight with two Fellowship questlines finished, and
   her ledger is the web's final knot.

The stone rolls back every night; the teachers do not. A questline
finished is finished for good — the one thing in the realm the stone
respects, because a Fellowship seat, once filled, stays filled.

## Decisions taken where this bible was vague

- **One-line steps.** A step is one pipe-separated line, like the
  phrasebook's rows: `step Name | desc | counter need | say | flag`.
  The flag is `cue` when the step serves on the E press, or
  `needs <counter>` when the realm does not count it yet.
- **The trial is one line too:** `trial Name | desc | counter need | line`.
- **Ties are prose**, one `ties <line>` each, not ids: the files are
  read by people first.
- **Art is bare rows** under a lone `art` word — the same rows the
  realm inks paint.
- **`power wish:fork 3`** names kind, id and level; the engine keeps
  `wish:fork`.
- **Where a teacher lives is the room id** (`room millpond`), CamelCase
  or hyphen; the engine maps both.
- **A waiting step** (`needs`) loads, is taught, and never finishes:
  the file itself says what the realm does not count yet.
