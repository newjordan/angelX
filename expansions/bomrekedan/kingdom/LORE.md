# Bomrekedan: a chronicle of the Kingdom of Meat Gods

Plain facts for writers and NPC dialogue. Sources: the live export of year 106
(`df-export/kingdom.json`) and the playthrough's gamelog. Where the two
disagree, the export wins, because the gamelog keeps stretches of play that
were later reloaded (in one of them a forgotten beast's dust killed a dozen
dwarves who are alive in the export).

## Founding

In the world of Anenu Ewè, the Planet of Enchanting, seven dwarves walked out
of the mountainhome Nefastasmel to make an outpost for their civilization, then
called The Strapping Merchant. Their leader was Kulet Semorathel. The site was
founded in the year 100 and named Bomrekedan, "Whipstirs". Eral Rakustëlot
was its first carpenter, and in the first months she made the fort's first
artifact, a ginkgo-wood armor stand, and offered it to The Misty Handles. Over
six years the outpost grew to 202 citizens, and Frank renamed it the Kingdom of
Meat Gods, of the Land of the Meat Gods. It is year 106 now.

Bomrekedan's wealth is close to five million: more than a million in
architecture, a million on display. It holds 3,546 buildings, 766 doors, 647
statues and 8,338 engravings.

## Rulers

- **Uvash Nakuthnish** ("Braidedtraded") was a miner when the dwarves elected her
  mayor in the first year. She was re-elected again and again, raised to
  baroness of Whipstirs, and issued mandates and export bans. Late in the fort's life
  she was possessed by a strange mood and made **The Cremation of Rogues**, a
  tetrahedrite millstone. Bomrekedan has no millstone workshop to set it in.
  She is still the baron.
- **Eigriteil Elmataskituged** ("Rainsuitors") is an erinys poet, 249 years old.
  She came as a visitor in the fort's first years and stayed. Soon after the
  rename the dwarves elected her mayor in Uvash's place. Her first act was a
  ban on exports.
- **Kulet Semorathel** ("Unionsringed"), the expedition leader, became manager,
  broker and bookkeeper at once. She made 30 masterpieces, among them beds,
  doors, cabinets and a native gold statue of the erinys bard Hulmul, and in a
  mood she made **The Lacy Portent**, a hematite crown.
- **Catten Egastshorast** ("Clenchwire") was a planter, then a farmer, then
  militia commander. He leads The Steel Guard.
- The other office-holders: Dungeon Master **Ùshrir Uzolog** (a woodcutter
  first), High Priest **Oddom Ferushat** ("first coal"), Priest **Solon
  Kolgídthur** ("sacred call"), Chief Medical Dwarf **Likot Tôsedèrith**,
  Messenger **Asmel Alåthcuggán**, and five militia captains.

## The peoples who live there

Of 202 citizens, 184 are dwarves. The rest came because of the taverns,
**The Brunch of Paddling** and **The Copper Peppers**, which draw bards, poets
and dancers from all over the world and keep some of them.

- **Erinyes** (11, of the Additional Races mod; the raws call them VALKYRIE).
  In the first year seven erinys warriors asked to live here and every petition
  was ignored. They kept coming anyway, as bards, dancers and poets, and they
  wrote books: four of the library's twelve codices are by erinyes, including
  *Elements of the Erinys* and *Uncovering the Erinys*. Now there are erinys
  residents who are warriors (a swordsman, an axeman, a maceman, a bowman), two
  erinys citizens, and an erinys mayor. They live for centuries: Skilmul
  Riddledqueens is 351.
- **Humans** (5). Two are citizens, the bard Saquar Clashesspeak and the old
  poet Ona Growledlined. Three are soldiers who came as visitors and stayed:
  the hammermen Ocgi Vesselbaby (80) and Pebu Tattooedmen and the pikeman Curo
  Grovetoasts. Human visitors now include a lady consort of the Nation of
  Cathedrals, a prophet and a guild alderperson.
- **Gnolls** (2), the poets Atututip Stretchedscorpions and the young Wuhpa
  Blackspurting, who is thirteen. The gnoll chief Pituta Jutuppititip visited
  twice and told tales from beyond Whipstirs. The log does not record when the
  two poets settled.
- **Temples for everyone.** Bomrekedan has 28 temples: to dwarf gods (Limâr the
  Jade Jewel, Datan the Ivory Gravel, Nökor the Fatal, Zim the Competitive,
  Avuz), to human gods, to five erinys gods, to an elf god (The Temple of
  Justice), to roc, stingray and basking shark gods, and three to **Gimis, a
  kobold**. The Strong Cult was recognised; the Ripe Cult's temple was
  abandoned.

## Wars

- **The ratfolk** were the first enemy and the most stubborn. In the first year
  an ambush of ratfolk axemen and thieves hit the fort. Later came ratfolk
  lashers with human macemen, then ratfolk thieves. In year 105 a siege of
  **The Seduction of Distinguishing**, led by the human **Nisam
  Shelterglossed**, brought ratfolk macemen, swordsmen and hammermen to the
  gate, and "shattered against Whipstirs". Ninety-one ratfolk lie dead on the
  map.
- **The goblins** sent thieves and snatchers. "Snatcher! Protect the children!"
  was called 22 times, and once a child, Gufirud Skardregra, "was seen being
  stolen". In year 104 an army of **The Unholy Nightmare** came seeking a
  parley under **Stozu Buryvice**, a commander who had once killed a dwarf
  named Kûbuk Claspmetal. The parley failed and the army shattered. Goblin
  crossbowmen ambushed the fort again near the end. Fifty-one goblins lie dead.
- **The caverns** sent troglodytes (25 dead), giant bats that wrestled them, and
  keas from the forest that stole gems and sandals (66 keas dead).

## Beasts

- **The giant Aca Vicárino Aleni Aca** came in the first year, "almost
  unparalleled in size". It lies dead on the map. The log never says who
  killed it.
- **Thirteen forgotten beasts** came up from below: Kok'tk Kok'tkdrik (iron),
  **Kekkit** (a towering feathered spider), Uggik, Itêg Ugoshråsh, Vrit,
  **Amas** (a gigantic eyeless sauropod with poison gas), Azoc, Pihyakh (green
  glass), **Kerleb the Savage Leap** (a winged blob of grime and filth whose
  frozen dust caught engravers, farmers and the surgeon), Skigeril Geggilgetil,
  R'kri, Tithleth Ostuk and Sodor. Twelve are dead. One is still alive on the
  map; the export does not say which.
- **Kekkit** killed Axe Lord **Monom Zontun** ("Helmsdoor"), who suffocated in
  its grip, and the armorer **Cerol Bomrekngotûn** ("Whippedcults") in year 104.
  It strangled the chief medical dwarf and the surgeon too, and they lived.
- **The Bronze Colossus Ùst Kezattaran Belal**, a magic statue bent on mayhem,
  came and fell; the log does not say how. Its bronze is still out there.
- **The deep.** Far below the halls (export z37, at the west map edge) seven
  fire wraiths and a gray demon lie dead, and four copper devils and a monster
  of fire are alive. They never reached the fort, and the log never names
  them.

Four dwarves are dead in the export: Monom and Cerol (Kekkit, 104), the tanner
**Thob Asënthabum** ("Gravelgulfs"), who bled to death after a giant bat caught
him (105), and the pikedwarf **Zuglar Mebzuthkesh** ("Oartails"), who drowned
(106). Eleven tombs stand, ten of them on z90.

## Artifacts

Forty-three. Thirteen came from strange moods, each offered to The Misty
Handles or kept as an heirloom: the armor stand, Sibrek Nishgomath's microcline
statue **Blamedrain the Everseeing Standards**, Ushat Âstshorast's morion flask
**Parchedwhisker the Friends of Color**, Erib Lorbamshameb's hazel grate, Zon
Tulonfath's apple-wood bracelet **Strangerfought the Frightful Smith**, Deduk
Erarfikod's limonite amulet, Kulet's crown, Ilral Ùstvutok's hematite mug, Dodók
Letmosging's wool tunic, Fath Kàslikot's iron scimitar, Oddom Olonnekut's hazel
bed, the baroness's millstone, and Urdim Keltomêm's iron gauntlet. One dwarf,
Astesh Èrithstukón, went mad in a mood in the log, but is sane in the export.
The last mood in the log took the clothier Alåth Nishmistêm, who claimed the
Clothier's Shop; how it ended is not recorded.

Seventeen are steel weapons and shields named by the soldiers who carried them
(Ineth Olinudib named six, Ùshrir Menginod four). Twelve are codices in the
library, written by naga, elf, goblin, erinys and dwarf authors: *The Struggle
Understood* (a naga's), *On the Kobolds* (an elf's), *The Rhythm: Fact or
Fiction?* (a goblin's), *Never Underestimate the Dwarf*. The last is an
instrument, **The Music of Diamond**, a puddingstone skufio.

Three dwarves were heard telling visitors about Strangerfought, Parchedwhisker
and The Music of Diamond. Those are the fort's own treasures, and thieves were
listening.

## The works

- **The machine works.** Thirty windmills stand on the hill (z100). Through 45
  gear assemblies and 48 axles they drive 52 screw pumps stacked from the
  surface down to z89, filling four wells and a pond. The world has no
  aquifers (a mod), so every drop is lifted. Erib Lorbamshameb's masterpiece
  pipe sections and Kogan Lelumletmos's enormous corkscrews are in it.
- **The workshop floor** (z97-98): 9 stoneworker's workshops (one of
  microcline), 8 stills, 4 kitchens, 4 carpenters, 4 mechanics, 2 jewelers, 2
  craftsdwarfs, a clothier, a butcher, a farmer's workshop, 8 farm plots.
- **The forge level** (z81): one smelter, two wood furnaces, one metalsmith's
  forge. One smelter had to make the coke, the pig iron and the steel for six
  steel squads, and that is why steel was short for years.
- **The showpieces**: a statue garden of 358 statues on z91; a platinum hall of
  119 platinum statues, 73 platinum tables and chairs and 51 platinum doors
  (17 more platinum doors and 10 gold statues are placed but unbuilt); a blue
  gate of 54 microcline doors and a microcline bridge; 21 display cases.
- **What it never had enough of**: fruit for the stills (the longest shortage,
  first summer to last line), cookable food (1,368 of 1,893 food items are
  turkey eggshells), steel bars, barrels, microcline, and in the early years
  refined coal. Some 28 dwarves have trades with no workshop: pressers, siege
  engineers, soap makers, weavers, a miller, a potter, a glassmaker, a bowyer.
